# Research notes (Sept 2026)

## Tool sharing: the idea being built
- **Has it been done?** Yes, many times. The survivors include Hygglo (which bought Fat Llama for about $41.5M in 2022; fees are about 20% from the owner and 10% from the renter), Yoodlize, Peerby, and small apps like ToolShed, ToolBevy, EzTool, Tooli and Sparetoolz.
- **Failures:** SnapGoods (2012) and outdoor-gear rental sites like GetOutfitted, Stokeshare and GearCommons. One analysis counts more than 120 peer-to-peer sharing platforms shut down since 2010.
- **Why they fail:** not enough listings close to renters, cheap items that aren't worth the hassle, and pickup/return friction.
- **Big-box stores:** Home Depot and Lowe's rent their own inventory only. AutoZone and O'Reilly lend specialty automotive tools (like spring compressors) free for a deposit.
- **Edge:**
  - AI bulk listing from a garage walkthrough, which attacks the supply problem
  - pro-grade and specialty gear
  - delivery, which widens the radius each owner can serve
  - "fits my batteries" filtering and battery add-ons
- **Liability:**
  - Platforms act as the middleman; the agreement is between owner and renter (Hygglo).
  - Damage and theft are covered by a platform guarantee (Yoodlize covers up to $2,000) plus ID checks and deposit holds.
  - Injury is the gap. Plan: a waiver at booking with safety acknowledgments for specific tools, condition photos at pickup and return, a quote for platform liability insurance, the most dangerous tools excluded at launch, and a warning to owners that homeowner policies may exclude rentals.
  - Get a lawyer and an insurance broker to review before launch.

## Chess Locator
- **Has it been done?** Yes: ChessMate, Chessmates, ChessNearby, nearChess and ChessBar. None dominant.
- **Hard parts:** finding a similar-skill player free at the same time nearby, players who won't pay, and self-reported ratings that are easy to game.
- **Better version:** scheduled "open board" sessions, revenue from venues, and Pokémon Go-style venue "gyms" as a feature for keeping players engaged.
- **Verdict:** fun, but weak as a business.

## Spotty (parking spot resale)
- **Already tried and banned:** MonkeyParking got a San Francisco cease-and-desist in 2014 ($300 fine per driver per transaction) and Los Angeles banned the category in 2015. Haystack was banned in Boston and elsewhere and shut down.
- **Legal alternatives:** free spot-swapping for credits, and renting private spots (Neighbor, JustPark, SpotHero). Both are crowded.
- **Verdict:** the original concept is legally dead.
